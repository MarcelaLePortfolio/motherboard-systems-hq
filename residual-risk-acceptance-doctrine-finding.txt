
RESIDUAL RISK ACCEPTANCE DOCTRINE FINDING

Finding Status: RECONCILED

Core Doctrine

Primary Objective:

Achieve intended future state.

Secondary Objective:

Minimize unnecessary risk.

Tertiary Objective:

Expose residual risk to the user.

Quaternary Objective:

Require explicit human acceptance of residual risk before authorization.

Meaning

Authorization is not merely confirmation that a proposal is understood.

Authorization is explicit acceptance of the residual risk associated with the proposed future state.

This creates a meaningful distinction between proposal review and execution authorization.

Lifecycle Interpretation

Interpretation Review:

  Did the system understand the user's intent?

Future-State Review:

  Is the proposed future state desired?

Risk Disclosure:

  What residual risk remains?

Authorization:

  Is the responsible human authority willing to accept that residual risk?

Architectural Implication

Risk visibility is not solely a safety feature.

Risk visibility is also an authorization-boundary feature.

Risk disclosure helps establish a distinct transition between:

- understanding a proposal

and

- accepting responsibility for proceeding

Governance Implication

Residual risk should not be hidden from the authorization corridor.

Residual risk should be visible at the decision point.

Supporting risk analysis may be progressively disclosed through expandable details.

Responsibility Boundary

The system proposes.

The system attempts to minimize unnecessary risk.

The system discloses residual risk.

The human authority determines whether residual risk is acceptable.

Relationship To Prior Findings

This finding reinforces:

Meaning

A proposal may be understood without being authorized.

A proposal may be desirable without being authorized.

Authorization occurs only after residual risk is made visible and consciously accepted.

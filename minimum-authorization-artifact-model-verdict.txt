Verdict:

The four future authorization surfaces should remain conceptually distinct:

1. Proposal Risk Surface

2. Residual Risk Disclosure Surface

3. Human Risk Acceptance Surface

4. Proposal-Bound Authorization Surface

Reason:

Each surface answers a different governance question.

Proposal Risk Surface asks:

What risk is attached to this exact proposal?

Residual Risk Disclosure Surface asks:

Was that risk visibly disclosed at the decision point?

Human Risk Acceptance Surface asks:

Did the responsible human authority accept the disclosed residual risk?

Proposal-Bound Authorization Surface asks:

Was execution authority granted for this exact proposal?

Boundary:

These concepts should not be collapsed semantically.

However, this finding does not require four separate database tables, files, UI panels, or runtime modules.

Future implementation may choose combined storage if lifecycle meaning, references, validity, and audit reconstruction remain distinct.

Primary Rule:

Storage may consolidate.

Lifecycle meaning must not collapse.

Architecture Rule:

Risk classification is not disclosure.

Disclosure is not acceptance.

Acceptance is not authorization.

Authorization is not execution.

Proposal identity must bind all four.

Audit Meaning:

A future audit should be able to reconstruct:

- what proposal was under review

- what risk was assigned

- what risk was shown to the user

- whether the user accepted that risk

- whether execution authority was granted

- whether the authorization matched the exact proposal executed

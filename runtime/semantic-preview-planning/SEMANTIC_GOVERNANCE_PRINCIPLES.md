PRINCIPLE 1 — Runtime Authority Supremacy

Authoritative runtime systems always retain final authority over execution, orchestration, rendering, Preview behavior, routing, retries, and persistence.

PRINCIPLE 2 — Semantic Descriptive Limitation

Semantic systems exist to observe and describe runtime-adjacent state only. Semantic systems do not govern runtime behavior.

PRINCIPLE 3 — Governance Before Influence

No semantic influence experimentation may occur before governance doctrine, rollback anchors, containment rules, and authority boundaries are explicitly defined.

PRINCIPLE 4 — Containment Preservation

All semantic systems must remain:

- developer-only

- markdown-only

- observational

- additive

- rollback-safe

- renderer-independent

- execution-independent

- orchestration-independent

- persistence-independent

- non-authoritative

PRINCIPLE 5 — Authority Separation

Semantic systems and runtime systems must remain constitutionally separated at all times.

PRINCIPLE 6 — Recoverability

All semantic governance evolution must remain:

- versioned

- tagged

- rollback-anchored

- disaster-backed-up

- checkpoint recoverable

PRINCIPLE 7 — Anti-Escalation

Semantic systems may never:

- acquire execution authority

- acquire orchestration authority

- acquire renderer authority

- acquire Preview authority

- rank runtime execution

- steer runtime behavior

- mutate runtime systems

- become advisory authority

PRINCIPLE 8 — Governance Stability

Governance doctrine must evolve more slowly and more conservatively than semantic capability expansion.

Semantic governance exists to preserve runtime integrity while safely enabling semantic observability evolution under constitutional containment.

Observed examples include:

## Candidate Hypothesis

Structural separation may improve evidence alignment by reducing construct conflation.

## Candidate Mechanism

When multiple distinct activities are grouped under a single construct:

- Scope may expand unnecessarily.

- Evidence may become ambiguous.

- Contradictions may be harder to identify.

- Reconciliation pressure may diffuse.

Structural separation may:

- Narrow scope.

- Clarify boundaries.

- Improve topology.

- Improve correspondence between claims and available evidence.

## Observed Pattern

The recurring sequence appears to be:

Conflated Construct

        ↓

## Evidence-Constrained Interpretation

Current evidence supports preservation of the recurring pattern.

Current evidence does not establish:

- That structural separation is universally beneficial.

- That all conflations should be separated.

- That structural separation always improves models.

- That reconciliation requires structural separation.

- That the observed pattern generalizes beyond the current investigation environment.

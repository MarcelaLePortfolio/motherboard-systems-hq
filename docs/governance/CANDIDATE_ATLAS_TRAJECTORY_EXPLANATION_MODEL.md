
# Atlas Trajectory Explanation Model

## Question

How might Atlas explain observed trajectories while remaining consistent with established relationship-cartography and observability governance?

---

## Candidate Model

Current evidence suggests a possible distinction between:

Trajectory

and

Trajectory Explanation.

A trajectory may exist within continuity independently of observation.

Atlas may identify and explain trajectories rather than creating them.

---

## Candidate Structure

Relationship

        ↓

Continuity

        ↓

Trajectory

        ↓

Trajectory Explanation

---

## Explanatory Checkpoints

Current investigation suggests that explanatory checkpoints may play a central role in trajectory explanation.

Examples include:

- Distinctions

- Findings

- Reconciliations

- Significant state transitions

These checkpoints appear to increase explanatory power regarding how a present state emerged.

---

## Candidate Atlas Role

Atlas may:

- Observe evidence-backed relationships

- Preserve continuity

- Identify trajectories

- Explain trajectories

Atlas may not:

- Create trajectories

- Create relationships

- Infer hidden cognition as fact

- Exceed established authority boundaries

---

## Metadata Relationship

Interaction metadata may contribute to trajectory explanation.

Examples may include:

- Intervention frequency

- Correction frequency

- Expansion frequency

- Participation patterns

- Reconciliation timing

Current evidence suggests metadata may help explain observed trajectories rather than create or authorize them.

---

## Evidence-Constrained Interpretation

Current evidence supports preservation of this model.

Current evidence does not establish:

- Atlas trajectory ontology

- Atlas intelligence derivation model

- Atlas runtime behavior

- Atlas implementation requirements

- Atlas final architectural role


# Lineage Preservation Rules

Status: ACTIVE GOVERNANCE SAFEGUARD

## Purpose

Preserve historical continuity, auditability, evidence lineage, and reconciliation history.

The purpose of lineage-preserving artifacts is not merely to store current conclusions.

Their purpose is to preserve how conclusions emerged.

Loss of lineage is treated as loss of governance evidence.

---

## Lineage-Preserving Artifacts

Examples include:

- Interpretation Evidence Ledgers (IELs)

- Evidence Ledgers

- Reconciliation Artifacts

- Investigation Histories

- Audit Trails

- Corridor Histories

- Governance Research Ledgers

This list is illustrative rather than exhaustive.

Any artifact whose primary purpose is preserving historical reasoning, evidence, interpretation lineage, or reconciliation history should be treated as lineage-preserving.

---

## Default Mutation Rule

Lineage-preserving artifacts default to append operations.

Historical entries should remain intact unless explicit authority exists to modify, archive, reconcile, or remove them.

The default action is:

Preserve existing lineage and extend it.

Not:

Replace existing lineage with a newer state.

---

## Replacement Authority Rule

Full-file replacement of lineage-preserving artifacts requires explicit justification.

Replacement should not occur solely because new evidence, findings, hypotheses, or observations have been generated.

Replacement requires a documented rationale explaining:

- why preservation is insufficient

- why replacement is necessary

- how lineage continuity will be maintained

---

## Recovery Principle

When lineage is unintentionally removed:

1. Stop further mutation.

2. Determine the last known good state.

3. Restore historical lineage.

4. Reapply intended additions through lineage-preserving operations.

5. Preserve recovery history.

Recovery is preferred over silent loss.

---

## Governance Observation

This safeguard was introduced after an evidence ledger was unintentionally replaced through a full-file overwrite operation.

The incident demonstrated that lineage-preserving artifacts require explicit mutation constraints distinct from ordinary operational documents.

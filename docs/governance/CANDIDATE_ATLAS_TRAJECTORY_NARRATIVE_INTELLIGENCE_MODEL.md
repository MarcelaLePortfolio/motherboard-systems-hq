
# Atlas Trajectory Narrative Intelligence Model

## Question

How should trajectory, narrative, and intelligence be distinguished within emerging Atlas continuity-cartography investigations?

---

## Candidate Model

Current investigation suggests that trajectory, narrative, and intelligence may represent distinct layers rather than interchangeable concepts.

Candidate structure:

Relationship

        ↓

Continuity

        ↓

Trajectory

        ↓

Narrative

        ↓

Intelligence

---

## Candidate Definitions

### Relationship

Answers:

What is connected?

---

### Continuity

Answers:

What remained connected over time?

---

### Trajectory

Answers:

What path emerged?

Trajectory represents an observed pattern of movement across continuity.

---

### Narrative

Answers:

How can the path be explained?

Narrative represents explanation of an observed trajectory.

Narrative may organize significant checkpoints, state transitions, and contextual factors into an understandable account of how the trajectory unfolded.

---

### Intelligence

Answers:

What can be learned from the path?

Intelligence represents generalized learning derived from explained trajectories.

Potential examples include:

- readiness assessment

- significance assessment

- organizational learning

- future pattern recognition

---

## Candidate Distinctions

Current investigation suggests:

Trajectory

        ≠

Narrative

Trajectory describes a path.

Narrative explains a path.

---

Narrative

        ≠

Intelligence

Narrative explains a specific path.

Intelligence derives potentially reusable learning from one or more explained paths.

---

## Metadata Relationship

Current evidence suggests interaction metadata may contribute primarily to narrative construction and trajectory explanation.

Current evidence does not establish that metadata creates, authorizes, or determines trajectories.

---

## Evidence-Constrained Interpretation

Current evidence supports preservation of these distinctions as a candidate organizing model.

Current evidence does not establish:

- Atlas ontology

- Atlas runtime behavior

- Atlas implementation requirements

- Atlas intelligence architecture

- Atlas final organizational role


# Candidate Collaboration Architecture Framework

Status: CANDIDATE

Owner: Atlas

Classification: Architectural Investigation

## Origin

This investigation originated from a Candidate V3 lineage inquiry.

During pressure-testing, the investigation shifted toward a possible architectural decomposition of collaboration itself.

The primary outcome was not resolution of the V3 question.

The primary outcome was identification of candidate collaboration layers.

---

## Candidate Layers

### Accessibility Layer

Examples:

- Natural-language restatement

- Contextual anchors

- Orientation support

- Reduced translation burden

- Reduced scrolling

- Reduced repetition

Candidate Purpose:

Reach reasoning.

---

### Reasoning Layer

Examples:

- Pressure testing

- Counterexample generation

- Distinction formation

- Scrutiny

- User-led correction

- Uncertainty preservation

- Reconciliation analysis

Candidate Purpose:

Produce insight.

---

### Governance Layer

Examples:

- Ledgers

- Hypotheses

- Findings

- Reconciliations

- Case studies

- Atlas organizational intelligence

Candidate Purpose:

Preserve organizational learning.

---

## Independent Failure Tests

### Accessibility Success / Reasoning Failure

Natural language and orientation succeed.

Scrutiny and challenge fail.

Result:

Easy but shallow corridor.

---

### Reasoning Success / Governance Failure

Insight is produced.

Learning is not preserved.

Result:

Good corridor.

Lost organizational learning.

---

### Governance Success / Accessibility Failure

Artifacts are preserved.

Participation becomes difficult.

Result:

Knowledge exists.

Reasoning accessibility degrades.

---

## Candidate Assessment

Current evidence suggests that collaboration activity may naturally separate into:

- Accessibility

- Reasoning

- Governance

The model currently explains:

- Historical V2 findings

- Recent orientation findings

- Atlas preservation findings

- Independent failure scenarios

---

## Constraints

This framework is not reconciled.

This framework remains a candidate architectural model.

Additional pressure testing is required.


## Additional Preserved Observation

A separate preserved observation suggests that participation frequency and participation influence may not be equivalent constructs.

Observed corridor history contains periods of reduced intervention frequency while still exhibiting participant interventions that materially altered investigation direction.

This observation remains unreconciled and should not currently be interpreted as evidence that participation frequency is unimportant.

Potential future relevance:

- Collaboration function-set determination.

- Collaboration quality measurement.

- Influence versus participation modeling.

- Accessibility / Reasoning / Governance framework evaluation.


# Candidate Observability Scope Corridor

Status: OPEN

## Origin

This corridor emerged while evaluating whether Atlas requires explicit directives to observe its own topology.

Subsequent discussion suggested the underlying question may be broader than Atlas and may apply to observability systems generally.

## Primary Question

What is the relationship between observability and self-observability?

## Candidate Questions

### Scope Questions

- Does observability naturally include the observer?

- Must self-observability be explicitly granted?

- Can self-observability be inherited from broader observability scope?

- What organizational constructs require self-observability?

### Boundary Questions

- What constitutes self-observation?

- What constitutes self-topology awareness?

- What metadata should be generated regarding the observer itself?

- Should self-generated metadata be treated differently than externally generated metadata?

### Risk Questions

- How can self-observability be achieved without creating recursive analysis loops?

- How can observer blind spots be identified?

- What risks emerge from excluding the observer?

- What risks emerge from over-prioritizing self-analysis?

### Architectural Questions

- Is self-observability a capability?

- Is self-observability a scope property?

- Is self-observability a governance decision?

- Is self-observability an architectural invariant?

Current evidence supports preservation of the corridor.

Current evidence does not establish:

- Runtime behavior requirements.

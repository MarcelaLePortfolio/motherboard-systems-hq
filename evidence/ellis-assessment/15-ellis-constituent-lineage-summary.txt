
ELLIS CONSTITUENT LINEAGE SUMMARY

Finding:

The Ellis constituent lineage inspection strengthens the corrected conclusion that Ellis is a named organizational authority with deferred implementation, not a merely mechanical router or service boundary.

Evidence observed:

- Milestone 1A and 1B show a pattern where named authority domains may stabilize even when implementation remains deferred.

- Atlas and Effie authority domains were explicitly stabilized while implementation remained deferred.

- HEADQUARTERS_ORGANIZATIONAL_CHARTER places Ellis in the same organizational role structure as Matilda, Cade, Effie, Atlas, Governance Validation, and User Authority.

- Governance Validation is modeled as Governance Office / Operationalization Review.

- Ellis is modeled separately as Operations Coordination / Operational Assignment and Coordination.

- Ellis owns assignment.

- Governance Validation owns operationalization review.

- Ellis determines ownership.

- Governance Validation determines requirements.

- Validator milestones show the system can define an important unnamed authority boundary without turning it into a named constituent identity.

Interpretation:

The naming and placement of Ellis are not incidental.

Ellis appears to have been introduced to fill a distinct organizational layer between Governance Validation and Operational Departments.

That layer is assignment, ownership, routing state, and coordination state.

Current conclusion:

Ellis constituent authority is supported.

Ellis runtime is not implemented.

Ellis implementation shape remains deferred.

Existing router, scheduler, task-state, and worker-claim primitives are downstream mechanics, not Ellis itself.

Future work should inspect implementation shape only after preserving this authority distinction.


ELLIS IMPLEMENTATION SHAPE CONCLUSION

Corridor Status:

CLOSED

Question:

What implementation shape is most consistent with Ellis' authority?

Summary:

The implementation-shape corridor began with the hypothesis that Ellis could remain a deterministic service boundary over assignment, routing, and ownership.

Pressure-testing progressively reduced unnecessary reasoning through coordination mechanisms, including:

- invocation instead of continuous monitoring

- operational reporting

- scheduled checkpoints

- governance escalation

- dependency awareness

These mechanisms significantly reduced implementation complexity and eliminated several previously-assumed reasoning responsibilities.

However, after exhausting reasonable coordination mechanisms, repository-consistent operational scenarios continued to produce contextual coordination ambiguities that could not be reduced to a stable deterministic rule set.

Examples included:

- resource contention

- assignment arbitration

- ownership arbitration

- routing ambiguity

- dependency-chain arbitration

- operational deadlock resolution

Finding:

Ellis' authority is not fundamentally justified by assignment.

Ellis is fundamentally justified by Operational Coordination Authority.

Assignment, ownership, routing, and operational arbitration are subordinate authorities exercised in service of maintaining coherent execution within the authorized capability envelope.

Implementation Finding:

Operational Coordination Authority appears to govern an open-ended space of contextual arbitration that is not realistically enumerable into a complete deterministic rule set.

Accordingly, the implementation shape most consistent with Ellis' authority is:

A bounded, invoked Operational Coordination Agent.

Operational Boundaries:

Ellis:

- does not interpret user intent

- does not modify governance

- does not define capabilities

- does not execute work

- does not continuously supervise execution

Ellis:

- is invoked when coordination authority is required

- gathers operational information as needed

- performs bounded operational arbitration

- resolves assignment, routing, ownership, and coordination ambiguity

- escalates governance defects to Governance Validation

Conclusion:

Repository evidence now supports Ellis as an invoked agent whose reasoning authority is bounded by Operational Coordination Authority.

This conclusion supersedes the earlier service-first implementation hypothesis while preserving all previously stabilized authority boundaries.

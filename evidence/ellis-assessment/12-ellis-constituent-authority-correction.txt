
ELLIS CONSTITUENT AUTHORITY CORRECTION

Finding:

Further inspection shows Ellis should not be reduced to a mechanical routing service boundary.

Repository governance documents identify Ellis as a named organizational constituent authority:

- HEADQUARTERS_ORGANIZATIONAL_CHARTER defines Operations Coordination with Role: Ellis.

- Ellis authority domain is Operational Assignment and Coordination.

- Ellis responsibilities include consuming Envelope artifacts, routing work by capability, assigning ownership, tracking operational status, and coordinating multi-agent workflows.

- Ellis produces Assignment, Ownership, Routing State, and Coordination State.

- CANONICAL_PACKAGE_SPECIFICATION states assignment belongs to Ellis and routing belongs to Ellis.

- CANONICAL_ENVELOPE_SPECIFICATION states the Envelope is routed and assigned by Ellis.

- GOVERNANCE_LIFECYCLE_STATE_MODEL defines ASSIGNED as Ellis having resolved capabilities and assigned ownership.

- GOVERNANCE_VALIDATION_SPECIFICATION states Governance Validation determines requirements while Ellis determines ownership.

Correction:

The absence of an implemented Ellis runtime proves only that implementation was deferred.

It does not prove Ellis was intended as a mere utility, service, or router primitive.

Current corrected conclusion:

Ellis is a specified named organizational authority for Operational Assignment and Coordination.

Ellis runtime is not implemented.

Ellis implementation shape remains unresolved and should not be collapsed prematurely into either:

- a mechanical service only, or

- an autonomous agent implementation now.

Future implementation should preserve Ellis as the authority responsible for transforming governance-derived requirements into assignment, ownership, routing state, and coordination state.

Current implementation recommendation:

Do not implement Ellis yet without explicit authorization.

Do not remove or weaken Ellis's constituent authority status.

Do not treat existing router/scheduler/task-state/worker-claim primitives as Ellis itself.

Treat those primitives as downstream mechanics that may eventually consume Ellis-owned assignment and coordination outputs.
